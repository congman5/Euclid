### The Existing Ecosystem

**The short version:** the mathematical work of deriving Euclid from Tarski is almost entirely done. It exists in multiple proof assistants. Your project is not starting from scratch — it is navigating an already-mapped territory and choosing which existing work to build on or translate from.

---

### 1. GeoCoq (Coq) — The Primary Reference

GeoCoq is the main formalization of geometry in the Coq proof assistant, containing both proofs about the foundations of geometry and high-level proofs. Its main development formalizes the first part of the Schwabhäuser-Szmielew-Tarski book *Metamathematische Methoden in der Geometrie* (1983), chapter by chapter, building from Tarski's 11 axioms upward.

The GeoCoq library now contains over 2800 lemmas and 100,000 lines of code.

For your specific goal, GeoCoq has two parallel tracks:

The first approach formalizes Euclid's statements without trying to formalize the original proofs, minimizing assumptions — for example using Gupta's proof that midpoint can be constructed without circle-circle continuity axioms. The second approach tries to formalize the original proofs of Euclid, filling gaps and reordering where necessary. The formalization of Book I was completed by Michael Beeson and exported to Coq by Julien Narboux.

The chapter files you need are all public on GitHub at `github.com/GeoCoq/GeoCoq`:
- `Tarski_dev/Ch02_cong.v` through `Ch13_3.v` — the Tarski foundation
- `Elements/` — the Euclid Book I propositions built on top

---

### 2. IsaGeoCoq (Isabelle/HOL) — A Full Port

Roland Coghetto ported GeoCoq chapters 2 through 13 into Isabelle/HOL. While GeoCoq's Coq proofs are written in procedural tactic style, the Isabelle transcript is done in Isar, which is Isabelle's declarative proof language — structurally much closer to Fitch-style reasoning than Coq tactics are.

This is directly relevant to you. Isar proofs look like:

```
proof -
  have "Bet A M B" using ...
  moreover have "Cong A M M B" using ...
  ultimately show ?thesis by ...
qed
```

The IsaGeoCoq port covers files Chap02.v to Chap13_3.v and suma.v, as well as associated definitions and files for parallel postulate proofs. It is available in the Isabelle Archive of Formal Proofs.

This is the closest existing work to what you are building — a declarative, line-by-line derivation from Tarski's axioms through to geometric theorems.

---

### 3. LeanEuclid (Lean 4) — System E in Lean

LeanEuclid implements System E directly in Lean 4, using SMT solvers to handle diagrammatic reasoning automatically. It has manually formalized all 48 propositions of Euclid's Elements Book I. The formalized proofs are faithful to Euclid's originals in the sense that they correspond closely to Euclid's own arguments.

LeanEuclid embeds System E's axioms as SMT commands, with a custom translator converting Lean's tactic states into SMT goals. It represents a pioneering effort in formalizing plane geometry within Lean.

The key limitation for your purposes: LeanEuclid sits at the System E level and uses SMT to discharge diagrammatic reasoning automatically. It does not derive System E's axioms from Tarski — it takes them as given and outsources the work to a solver. So it is not what you want as a foundation, but it is excellent evidence that Lean 4 is a viable target language.

---

### 4. LeanGeo (Lean 4) — Extended System E

LeanGeo builds on LeanEuclid and extends it with 52 new geometric definitions and 260 manually proved theorems, covering material through competition-level geometry. It also uses CVC5 as its SMT backend for diagrammatic reasoning steps.

Again, this is at the System E level, not derived from Tarski.

---

### 5. GeoLean (Lean 3/Mathlib) — Tarski in Lean

A partial Lean port of GeoCoq covering approximately 70% of the Tarski development exists in the GeoLean branch of mathlib3, though it was never merged into mathlib proper. The GeoLean project was designed as a direct port of the Coq version rather than a Lean-style development, and the main issue is that the Coq style does not work well with the mathlib style. There is currently no complete, clean Lean 4 port of the Tarski-to-Euclid derivation.

---

### 6. HOL Light — Beeson's Proofs

The Beeson-Narboux-Wiedijk formalization was also exported to HOL Light in addition to Coq. HOL Light uses a different style again — closer to a sequent calculus — but the mathematical content is identical to GeoCoq's Elements directory.

---

### 7. Metamath and Mizar — Fragment Coverage

Both have partial formalizations of Tarski geometry. Neither has a complete Book I coverage comparable to GeoCoq.

### What This Means for Your Project

The landscape has a clear gap that your project could fill:

| What exists | What does not exist |
|---|---|
| Tarski → Euclid in Coq (GeoCoq, ~100k lines) | Tarski → Euclid in a clean Fitch-style system |
| Tarski → Euclid in Isabelle/Isar (IsaGeoCoq) | A human-readable, pedagogically transparent derivation |
| System E in Lean 4 (LeanEuclid, SMT-backed) | Tarski → System E → Euclid fully explicit in Lean 4 |
| Partial Lean 3 port of GeoCoq | A complete Lean 4 Tarski foundation |

Your project — a Fitch-style proof system deriving everything from Tarski's 11 axioms through System E to Euclid's propositions — does not exist in any completed form. The mathematical content is all in GeoCoq. The translation to explicit, human-readable, Fitch-style natural deduction has not been done.

The practical recommendation is to use **GeoCoq as your mathematical oracle** — every lemma you need to prove has already been proved there, so you can check your proofs against GeoCoq's results — while building your own Fitch-style presentation on top. The IsaGeoCoq Isar proofs are worth reading because Isar's declarative style is the closest existing thing to what you are building, and many of those proofs can be adapted fairly directly into a Fitch format.
